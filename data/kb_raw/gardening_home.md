# domains(id|name|definition)
DM1|in-ground gardening|traditional cultivation in native or amended soil; beds, rows, or mounds directly in earth
DM2|raised bed gardening|contained growing above ground level; framed beds filled with custom soil mix; improved drainage and ergonomics
DM3|container gardening|growing in pots, buckets, grow bags, or other portable vessels; suitable for patios, balconies, rooftops, and limited space
DM4|hydroponics|soilless growing in nutrient solution; roots in water, inert media, or misted; precise nutrient control
DM5|aquaponics|integrated fish + plant system; fish waste provides nutrients for plants; plants filter water for fish; closed-loop
DM6|vertical gardening|growing on vertical surfaces or stacked layers; maximizes production per ground area; walls, towers, trellises, stacked planters
DM7|indoor gardening|growing inside structures under artificial or window light; year-round production independent of outdoor climate
DM8|permaculture food systems|design-based approach: perennial polycultures, guilds, food forests, swales, integrated livestock; mimics natural ecosystem
DM9|microgreens and sprouting|very young seedlings (7–21 days) or germinated seeds (2–5 days); grown in trays indoors; minimal space; fast harvest

# concepts(id|name|definition|category)
GC1|no-dig / no-till|method: do not disturb soil structure; add compost and mulch on surface; worms and microbes incorporate; preserves soil food web, fungal networks, and structure; reduces weed germination (weed seeds stay buried)|method
GC2|soil food web|ecosystem of organisms in soil: bacteria, fungi (especially mycorrhizal), protozoa, nematodes, arthropods, earthworms; decompose organic matter, cycle nutrients, build soil structure, suppress disease; healthy soil = healthy plants|principle
GC3|crop rotation|sequential planting of different plant families in each bed across years; prevents disease/pest buildup; manages soil fertility (legumes before heavy feeders); minimum 3-year; ideally 4–5 year|principle
GC4|succession planting|sowing same crop at regular intervals to ensure continuous harvest rather than single glut; most important for fast crops (lettuce, radish, bean, cilantro); interval = days-to-maturity / 3 roughly|technique
GC5|companion planting|placing specific plants near each other for mutual benefit: pest deterrence, pollinator attraction, nitrogen fixation, physical support, ground covering; based on empirical observation and some science|technique
GC6|integrated pest management (IPM)|hierarchical approach: prevention (rotation, resistant varieties, sanitation) → cultural controls (spacing, water management) → biological controls (beneficial insects, Bt) → physical controls (row cover, hand-picking) → targeted organic sprays as last resort|principle
GC7|hardening off|gradual acclimatization of indoor-started seedlings to outdoor conditions over 7–10 days; increasing sun exposure, wind, and temperature variation daily; prevents transplant shock|technique
GC8|seed starting|growing plants from seed indoors or in protected environment before transplanting to garden; provides head start on growing season (4–10 weeks depending on crop); essential in short-season climates|technique
GC9|mulching|covering soil surface with organic material (straw, leaves, wood chips, compost) or inorganic material (plastic, landscape fabric); suppresses weeds, retains moisture, moderates soil temperature, feeds soil (organic); 5–10 cm organic mulch|technique
GC10|pH management|maintaining soil pH in optimal range (6.0–7.0 for most vegetables); pH determines nutrient availability; lime raises pH; sulfur lowers pH; test before amending; wood ash raises pH (contains potassium and calcium)|technique
GC11|frost date awareness|knowing average last spring frost and first fall frost dates for your location; determines planting timing, variety selection, and season extension needs; erring conservative (wait 1–2 weeks past average) reduces risk|planning
GC12|seed viability|duration seeds remain capable of germination under proper storage (cool, dry, dark); varies by species: onion 1–2 years, tomato 4–7 years, bean 3–5 years; test old seed with germination test (10 seeds on moist paper towel; count sprouted in 7–14 days)|knowledge
GC13|organic matter|decomposed plant and animal material in soil; drives soil fertility, water retention, structure, and biological activity; target 5%+ organic matter; add compost annually (2–5 cm top-dress); organic matter IS the garden|principle
GC14|perennial food systems|plants that produce food year after year without replanting: fruit trees, berry bushes, asparagus, rhubarb, horseradish, herbs, perennial onions (walking onion, Egyptian onion); lower annual labor than annuals; years to full production|system
GC15|food forest guilds|designed plant communities mimicking forest layers: canopy (fruit tree), understory (berry bush), herb layer (comfrey, mint), ground cover (strawberry, clover), root layer (garlic), vine (grape, kiwi); each element serves multiple functions|system
GC16|biological nitrogen fixation|legumes (Fabaceae) host Rhizobium bacteria in root nodules; bacteria convert atmospheric N₂ to plant-available NH₃; legume gets nitrogen; bacteria get carbon; 50–200 kg N/ha/season fixed; leave roots in soil after harvest to release N for next crop|mechanism
GC17|cover cropping|growing non-harvest crops (clover, rye, vetch, buckwheat, oats) to protect and improve soil between main crop seasons; prevents erosion; adds organic matter; fixes nitrogen (legume covers); suppresses weeds; feeds soil food web; cut/crimp/till before planting next crop|technique
GC18|vermicomposting|using redworms (Eisenia fetida) to convert organic waste to worm castings (richest compost); operates 15–25°C; processes waste continuously; produces castings and worm tea; ideal for apartments and small spaces|technique
