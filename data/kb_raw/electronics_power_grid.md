# domains(id|name|definition)
DM1|generation|conversion of primary energy (chemical, nuclear, kinetic, radiant, thermal) to electrical energy; centralized (utility-scale) and distributed (behind-the-meter); synchronous generators dominate conventional; inverter-based resources (IBR) growing
DM2|transmission|bulk transfer of electrical energy at high voltage (69–1,200 kV) over long distances (10–1,000+ km); AC (dominant) and HVDC; interconnects generators and load centers; operated by transmission operators (TO) and independent system operators (ISO/RTO)
DM3|distribution|delivery of electrical energy from transmission substations to end-use customers at medium voltage (4–35 kV) and low voltage (120–600 V); radial or network topology; operated by distribution utilities or cooperatives
DM4|grid operations and control|real-time balancing of generation and load; frequency regulation; voltage control; economic dispatch; contingency management; supervisory control and data acquisition (SCADA); energy management system (EMS)
DM5|electricity markets|wholesale and retail markets for energy, capacity, and ancillary services; price formation; dispatch optimization; financial instruments; regulatory structures (regulated/deregulated)
DM6|grid protection|detection and isolation of faults to prevent equipment damage, maintain stability, and ensure personnel safety; relays, breakers, fuses, reclosers, coordination
DM7|power quality|maintenance of voltage magnitude, frequency, waveform purity, and reliability at the point of delivery; harmonics, sags, swells, flicker, interruptions
DM8|renewable integration|connection of variable renewable energy (VRE: solar PV, wind) to the grid; challenges: variability, uncertainty, low inertia, voltage/frequency ride-through; solutions: storage, flexible generation, grid-forming inverters, forecasting
DM9|energy storage|devices and systems storing energy for later dispatch; balances supply-demand mismatch; provides ancillary services; time-shifting; arbitrage; resilience

# concepts(id|name|definition|category)
EC1|frequency-load balance|at every instant, total generation must equal total load + losses; surplus → frequency rises (generators accelerate); deficit → frequency falls (generators decelerate); frequency is the universal indicator of generation-load balance across entire interconnection|fundamental
EC2|reactive power and voltage|reactive power (VAR) does not perform real work but is essential for maintaining voltage and energizing magnetic fields (motors, transformers); generated by: overexcited generators, capacitors, STATCOM; absorbed by: underexcited generators, inductive loads, reactors; voltage is primarily controlled by reactive power balance (locally, not system-wide like frequency)|fundamental
EC3|power factor|PF = P/S = cos φ; ratio of real power (W) to apparent power (VA); unity PF: all power does useful work; low PF: excess current flows (wasted capacity, higher losses); inductive loads (motors) → lagging PF; capacitive → leading PF; corrected by adding capacitors (leading) or reducing reactive demand|fundamental
EC4|three-phase power|three sinusoidal voltages displaced 120° in phase; balanced three-phase: constant power delivery (no pulsation, unlike single-phase); enables rotating magnetic field (motors); more efficient copper use than single-phase for same power; transmission and distribution: 3-phase; residential service: split single-phase (120/240 V from single-phase transformer center-tapped secondary)|fundamental
EC5|per-unit system|normalization: actual values expressed as fraction of base value; per-unit = actual/base; bases: V_base (system voltage), S_base (chosen: typically 100 MVA), Z_base = V²/S, I_base = S/V; simplifies calculations across voltage levels (transformer ratios disappear in per-unit); typical: V = 1.0 pu, transformer impedance = 0.05–0.15 pu|analytical
EC6|fault level (short-circuit capacity)|maximum current (or MVA) that would flow at a point if a bolted three-phase fault occurred; indicates system strength; high fault level → stiff system (voltage recovers quickly, frequency stable); low fault level → weak system (voltage sensitive, IBR stability concerns); short-circuit ratio (SCR) = fault level (MVA) / IBR capacity (MVA); SCR < 3: weak grid|analytical
EC7|merit order dispatch|generators dispatched in order of marginal cost (cheapest first): nuclear and hydro and renewable (near-zero marginal cost) → coal → gas CCGT → gas peaker; market clearing price set by most expensive dispatched unit (marginal unit); renewable displaces fossil in merit order (merit order effect → lower wholesale prices)|market
EC8|locational marginal pricing (LMP)|wholesale energy price at each bus reflecting: marginal energy cost + marginal congestion cost + marginal loss cost; LMP varies by location when transmission is constrained; generators at constrained export locations receive lower LMP; load at constrained import locations pays higher LMP; drives efficient siting and investment signals|market
EC9|capacity factor|CF = actual energy produced / (rated capacity × time); indicates how much of theoretical maximum a generator actually produces; baseload: CF 80–95% (nuclear, coal, geothermal); mid-merit: 50–80% (CCGT); peaker: 5–30% (NGCT); wind: 25–55%; solar: 15–30%; high CF → lower LCOE (fixed costs spread over more MWh)|metric
EC10|LCOE (levelized cost of energy)|total lifecycle cost (capital + fuel + O&M + decommissioning + financing) divided by total lifetime energy production (MWh); expressed in $/MWh; enables comparison across technologies; limitations: doesn't capture value (dispatchable vs variable), location, or integration costs|metric
EC11|curtailment|forced reduction of renewable generation output below available capacity; causes: transmission congestion, minimum generation constraints (baseload units can't reduce further), oversupply; wasted energy; economic loss to generator; solutions: storage, flexible load, better transmission, better forecasting|operational
EC12|interconnection|tying together of multiple electric systems to operate synchronously; enables: resource sharing, reserve sharing, economic exchange, reliability improvement; North America: 3 interconnections (Eastern, Western, ERCOT) + Quebec; synchronous: all generators in interconnection are phase-locked at exactly same frequency|system
EC13|black start|restoring power after complete blackout without external electricity; black-start units (hydro, some gas turbines, batteries) start independently → energize transmission sections → sequentially start other generators → rebuild system; process: hours (small system) to days (large system); requires pre-planned restoration procedure|operational
EC14|demand response (DR)|customers reduce or shift electricity use in response to price signals, reliability signals, or utility programs; types: price-responsive (TOU, real-time pricing), incentive-based (direct load control, interruptible tariff, emergency DR); essentially: load as a resource; treated as supply-side in markets|operational
EC15|net metering|billing mechanism: customer with DER (typically solar PV) exports excess generation to grid; meter runs backward (or net calculation at billing cycle); credited at retail rate (full net metering) or reduced rate (net billing); controversial: cost shift debate (non-solar customers pay more for fixed grid costs); evolving to successor tariffs (value of DER, grid services)|regulatory
