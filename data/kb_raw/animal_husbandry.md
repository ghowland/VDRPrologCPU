# concepts(id|name|definition|category)
CO1|Ruminant|animal with four-chambered stomach (rumen, reticulum, omasum, abomasum); ferments cellulose via microbial symbiosis; cattle, goats, sheep|digestion
CO2|Monogastric|single-chambered stomach; cannot digest cellulose efficiently; pigs, chickens|digestion
CO3|Cud Chewing (Rumination)|ruminant regurgitates partially fermented feed, re-chews, re-swallows; 6-8 hours/day; essential for fiber breakdown and saliva buffering|digestion
CO4|Rumen Microbiome|bacteria, protozoa, fungi in rumen fermenting cellulose into volatile fatty acids (VFAs: acetate, propionate, butyrate); provides ~70% of ruminant energy|digestion
CO5|Volatile Fatty Acids (VFAs)|primary energy source for ruminants; acetate (energy, milk fat), propionate (glucose via liver), butyrate (rumen wall energy); ratio affected by diet|digestion
CO6|Crop (Poultry)|muscular pouch in esophagus storing food before gizzard; allows batch feeding; food softened before mechanical grinding|digestion
CO7|Gizzard|muscular organ grinding feed with ingested grit; replaces teeth in poultry; requires access to insoluble grit (granite, flint)|digestion
CO8|Body Condition Score (BCS)|standardized visual/tactile assessment of fat cover; scale varies by species (1-5 or 1-9); ideal BCS ensures health, fertility, production|assessment
CO9|Dry Matter Intake (DMI)|weight of feed consumed minus water content; basis for ration formulation; varies by body weight, production stage, feed quality|nutrition
CO10|Crude Protein (CP)|nitrogen content of feed × 6.25; includes true protein and non-protein nitrogen; ruminants can use NPN via rumen microbes; monogastrics need true protein|nutrition
CO11|Total Digestible Nutrients (TDN)|energy value of feed accounting for digestibility; sum of digestible protein + digestible fiber + digestible NFE + (digestible fat × 2.25)|nutrition
CO12|Forage|plant material (grass, hay, silage, browse) forming bulk of ruminant diet; provides fiber essential for rumen function; minimum 1% BW as forage for ruminants|nutrition
CO13|Concentrate|energy-dense, low-fiber feed; grains (corn, barley, oats), oilseed meals (soybean, linseed); supplements forage for high-producing animals|nutrition
CO14|Colostrum|first milk produced after birth; rich in immunoglobulins (IgG), fat, vitamins; passive immunity transfer; must be consumed within 6-12 hours of birth; gut absorption closes by 24 hours|reproduction
CO15|Passive Immunity|antibodies transferred from dam to offspring via colostrum; protects neonate until own immune system develops (~2-4 weeks); failure = increased disease susceptibility and mortality|health
CO16|Gestation|period from conception to birth; species-specific duration; nutritional demands increase in final third|reproduction
CO17|Lactation|milk production period following parturition; duration and yield species-dependent; peak production 4-8 weeks post-partum then gradual decline|reproduction
CO18|Estrus (Heat)|period of sexual receptivity in female; external signs vary by species; timing of breeding relative to estrus determines conception rate|reproduction
CO19|Weaning|transition from milk to solid feed; stressful period; gradual weaning reduces stress; timing affects dam recovery and offspring growth|reproduction
CO20|Parasite Load|internal (worms, coccidia) and external (lice, mites, ticks, flies) parasites; managed by rotation, sanitation, targeted treatment; resistance develops with overuse of chemical dewormers|health
CO21|Vaccination|stimulating immune response against specific pathogens before exposure; core vaccines species-specific; timing relative to age, season, and risk|health
CO22|Biosecurity|practices preventing disease introduction and spread: quarantine new arrivals (14-21 days), limit visitors, clean footwear, isolate sick animals, control wildlife contact|health
CO23|Herd/Flock Immunity|proportion of group immune to disease reducing transmission; achieved through vaccination and natural exposure; protects vulnerable individuals (young, immunocompromised)|health
CO24|Stress Response|physiological and behavioral reaction to stressor (transport, handling, weather, social disruption); cortisol release; immunosuppression; reduced production; increased disease susceptibility|welfare
CO25|Five Freedoms (Animal Welfare)|freedom from: hunger/thirst; discomfort; pain/injury/disease; fear/distress; freedom to express normal behavior; framework for welfare assessment|welfare
CO26|Pecking Order|social hierarchy in poultry established by aggression; determines access to feed, water, roost, nest; disrupted by adding/removing birds; stabilizes after 1-2 weeks|behavior
CO27|Herd Hierarchy|social dominance structure in cattle, goats, pigs; determines resource access; established by physical contests and maintained by learned submission; horned animals dominate hornless|behavior
CO28|Photoperiod|day length affecting reproductive cycling, molt (poultry), coat growth; poultry need 14-16 hours light for consistent laying; decreasing photoperiod triggers molt|environment
CO29|Thermoneutral Zone (TNZ)|temperature range where animal maintains body temperature without additional energy expenditure; outside TNZ: energy diverted from production to thermoregulation|environment
CO30|Heat Stress|body temperature rises above normal; panting, reduced feed intake, decreased production, fertility loss; cattle most susceptible among farm animals; shade, water, ventilation critical|environment
CO31|Cold Stress|ambient temperature below lower critical temperature; increased feed intake needed for thermogenesis; young animals most vulnerable; shelter, bedding, windbreak essential|environment
CO32|Zoonosis|disease transmissible from animals to humans; Q fever, brucellosis, salmonella, campylobacter, ringworm, cryptosporidium; hygiene and vaccination reduce risk|health
CO33|Mastitis|inflammation of mammary gland; bacterial infection most common cause; detected by changes in milk (clots, color, odor) and udder (heat, swelling, pain); leading health issue in dairy|health
CO34|Bloat|rumen distension from trapped gas (frothy or free-gas); life-threatening; caused by lush legume pasture (frothy) or esophageal obstruction (free-gas); emergency treatment required|health
CO35|Ketosis (Acetonemia)|metabolic disorder from negative energy balance; body mobilizes fat → excess ketone bodies; common in early lactation high-producing cows and late-pregnancy ewes/does (pregnancy toxemia)|health
CO36|Coccidiosis|protozoan infection (Eimeria spp.) of intestinal tract; bloody diarrhea in young animals; stress and overcrowding trigger outbreaks; develops immunity with controlled exposure|health
CO37|Foot Rot|bacterial infection of interdigital skin; lameness; caused by wet conditions + Fusobacterium necrophorum; prevention: dry footing, zinc sulfate foot baths, trimming|health
CO38|Impacted Crop|crop fails to empty; food mass hardens; caused by long fibrous material, dehydration, or crop muscle weakness; massage, oil, surgical intervention|health
CO39|Egg Binding|hen unable to pass formed egg; caused by calcium deficiency, oversized egg, obesity, dehydration; warm bath, calcium supplement, lubrication; veterinary intervention if persistent|health
CO40|Prolapse|protrusion of reproductive or digestive tissue; uterine (post-partum), vaginal (pre-partum), rectal, vent (poultry); emergency; clean, reduce, retain; may recur|health
