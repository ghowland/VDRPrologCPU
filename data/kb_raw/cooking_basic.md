# concepts(id|name|definition|category)
CO1|Maillard Reaction|non-enzymatic browning between amino acids and reducing sugars above ~140°C/280°F; produces hundreds of flavor compounds; requires dry surface and high heat; not caramelization (which is sugar-only)|chemistry
CO2|Caramelization|pyrolysis of sugars above ~170°C/340°F; produces brown color and complex bittersweet flavors; distinct from Maillard (no protein needed); sucrose caramelizes at ~160°C, fructose at ~110°C|chemistry
CO3|Emulsification|dispersing one liquid into another immiscible liquid (oil in water or water in oil) stabilized by emulsifier (lecithin in egg yolk, mustard, garlic); mayonnaise, vinaigrette, pan sauce|chemistry
CO4|Gelatinization|starch granules absorb water and swell when heated (60-80°C depending on starch); thickens liquids; irreversible; different starches gel at different temperatures|chemistry
CO5|Denaturation|protein unfolding from heat, acid, salt, or mechanical action; changes texture (egg whites solidify; meat firms); first step of cooking protein; overdenaturation = tough, dry|chemistry
CO6|Gluten Development|kneading wheat flour + water hydrates glutenin and gliadin proteins, which cross-link into elastic gluten network; traps gas (leavening); more kneading = more structure|chemistry
CO7|Fermentation (Culinary)|microbial metabolism converting sugars to acids, alcohol, or gas; leavens bread (CO₂); produces alcohol (ethanol); preserves (lactic acid); develops flavor complexity|chemistry
CO8|Osmosis (Salting/Brining)|salt draws water from cells via osmotic pressure; salting extracts moisture (curing, sweating vegetables); brining adds moisture and seasons interior (equilibrium brine)|chemistry
CO9|Acid-Base Balance|pH affects flavor (sour), texture (acid tenderizes, alkali toughens greens), color (red cabbage changes with pH), and reaction speed; balancing acid in finished dish = brightness|chemistry
CO10|Fat as Flavor Carrier|fat-soluble flavor compounds dissolve in fat; fat coats tongue increasing flavor perception; cooking fat transfers heat; fat in sauce creates body and mouthfeel|chemistry
CO11|Mise en Place|everything in its place; all ingredients measured, cut, and organized before cooking begins; prevents mistakes; enables speed; professional kitchen fundamental|practice
CO12|Seasoning (Salt)|salt enhances and amplifies existing flavors; suppresses bitterness; balances sweetness; season throughout cooking (not just at end); undersalted food tastes flat regardless of other flavors|practice
CO13|Tasting and Adjusting|continuous tasting during cooking; adjusting salt, acid, fat, heat, sweetness to balance; final seasoning most critical step; no recipe replaces palate|practice
CO14|Heat Control|managing temperature of cooking medium; high heat for searing/browning; medium for cooking through; low for braising/simmering; resting allows carryover cooking|practice
CO15|Knife Skills|cutting techniques determining piece size, shape, and uniformity; uniform cuts cook evenly; brunoise (3mm dice), small dice (6mm), medium dice (12mm), large dice (20mm), julienne (3mm strips), chiffonade (ribbons)|practice
CO16|Fond|browned bits stuck to pan bottom after searing; concentrated flavor from Maillard reaction; dissolved by deglazing with liquid (wine, stock, water); foundation of pan sauces|concept
CO17|Reduction|simmering liquid to evaporate water; concentrates flavor, body, and color; reduces volume; thickens naturally; essential sauce technique; over-reduction = too salty/intense|technique
CO18|Resting (Meat)|allowing cooked meat to sit before cutting; internal temperature equalizes; juices redistribute from center to exterior; carryover cooking continues (5-10°F rise); cutting too soon = juice loss|practice

# ingredients(id|name|category|flavor_role|key_properties|storage)
IG1|Salt (NaCl)|mineral|flavor enhancer; suppresses bitterness; balances sweetness; essential in every dish|draws moisture via osmosis; controls fermentation; preserves; dissolves in water; kosher salt preferred (no additives, easy to pinch)|indefinite dry storage
IG2|Black Pepper|spice|pungent heat (piperine); aromatic; universal seasoning paired with salt|freshly ground vastly superior to pre-ground (volatile oils dissipate rapidly); add during cooking for depth, at end for brightness; loses potency when cooked long|whole peppercorns: 2+ years; ground: 3-6 months peak
IG3|Onion|aromatic vegetable|sweetness when cooked (sulfur compounds convert to sugars); pungent raw; foundation of most savory cooking worldwide|yellow: all-purpose; white: sharper, Mexican/Asian; red: milder raw, salads; shallot: refined, French; cut onion releases syn-propanethial-S-oxide (tear gas)|cool, dark, dry; weeks to months
IG4|Garlic|aromatic vegetable|pungent raw (allicin); sweet and mellow when slow-roasted; ubiquitous across cultures; burns easily (bitter)|crush/mince activates allicin (let sit 10 min for maximum); burns above ~175°C (add after onion, cook briefly); paste for marinades; whole cloves for slow cooking|cool, dry, ventilated; weeks; not refrigerated
IG5|Ginger (Fresh)|aromatic rhizome|bright, sharp, warm heat; different compound from pepper or chili (gingerol → shogaol when dried); adds lift|grate on microplane for paste; slice for infusions; young ginger milder; pairs with garlic across Asia|refrigerated: 3 weeks; frozen (grated): months
IG6|Tomato|fruit/vegetable|acid + umami + sweetness; provides body when reduced; base of sauces worldwide; contains glutamate (umami)|fresh (summer peak); canned (San Marzano DOP or quality whole peeled year-round); paste (concentrated, triple umami); sun-dried (intense); never refrigerate fresh (destroys texture/flavor)|fresh: counter; canned: pantry years; paste: refrigerated after opening
IG7|Olive Oil|fat|fruity, peppery, grassy (extra virgin); neutral when refined; Mediterranean foundation|extra virgin: finishing, dressings, low-medium heat; refined/pure: higher heat cooking; smoke point EVOO ~190°C; quality varies enormously; taste before using|dark, cool; 12-18 months; rancidity = off flavors
IG8|Neutral Oil (Vegetable/Canola/Sunflower)|fat|minimal flavor; high smoke point; carrier for other flavors; frying medium|smoke point ~230°C; use for high-heat searing, frying, stir-frying; doesn't compete with other flavors|cool, dark; 6-12 months
IG9|Butter|fat|rich, nutty, creamy flavor; emulsifier (contains water, fat, milk solids); versatile|clarified/ghee: remove milk solids for higher smoke point (~250°C); whole butter: smoke point ~175°C (milk solids burn); browning butter (beurre noisette): nutty, complex flavor; salted vs unsalted (unsalted for cooking control)|refrigerated: months; frozen: year; ghee: pantry months
IG10|Egg|protein/emulsifier/leavener|binding, leavening, emulsifying, enriching, coating; most versatile single ingredient|yolk: emulsifier (lecithin), richness, color; white: protein foam (meringue), binding, clarity; whole: custard, scrambled, binding; room temperature for best whipping/emulsifying|refrigerated: 3-5 weeks; freshness test: sinks in water = fresh; floats = old
IG11|Wheat Flour (All-Purpose)|starch/protein|structure from gluten; thickening from starch; coating for frying; base of bread, pasta, pastry|protein ~10-12% (AP); higher protein = more gluten = chewier; lower = more tender; bleached vs unbleached (minimal practical difference for most uses); measure by weight not volume for accuracy|cool, dry; 6-12 months; whole wheat: refrigerate (oils go rancid)
IG12|Rice|grain|neutral starchy base; absorbs flavor; staple for half the world|long grain (fluffy, separate: basmati, jasmine); medium grain (slightly sticky: arborio for risotto); short grain (sticky: sushi); brown: whole grain, nuttier, longer cook; 1:1.5 to 1:2 rice:water ratio varies by type|dry: years; cool, dry; cooked: refrigerate 3-5 days
IG13|Lemon / Lime|acid fruit|brightness; acidity balances richness; cuts fat; enhances salt perception; zest adds oil-based aroma without liquid|juice: acid component; zest: aromatic oil (limonene); add at end for brightness, during cooking for integration; acid tenderizes protein (ceviche); prevents browning (apple, avocado)|refrigerated: 2-4 weeks; juice freezes well
IG14|Vinegar|acid|sharpness; preservation; deglazing; dressing; pickling; various types provide different flavor profiles|wine vinegar (subtle, European); rice vinegar (mild, Asian); apple cider (fruity, all-purpose); distilled white (pickling, cleaning); balsamic (sweet-acid, finishing); sherry vinegar (complex, Spanish)|pantry: years (does not spoil)
IG15|Soy Sauce|fermented condiment|salt + umami + color + depth; fermented soybeans + wheat + salt; universal Asian seasoning|light/thin: saltier, lighter color, cooking; dark/thick: less salty, more color, finishing; tamari: wheat-free; use as salt substitute for added depth; naturally brewed vs chemically hydrolyzed (huge quality difference)|pantry: 2-3 years; refrigerate after opening for best quality
IG16|Chili (Dried/Fresh/Flake)|spice|heat (capsaicin) + flavor (each variety has distinct flavor beyond heat); dried have deeper, more complex flavor than fresh|fresh jalapeño/serrano: green heat, vegetal; dried ancho: fruity, mild; chipotle: smoky; gochugaru: fruity, Korean; Aleppo: fruity, warm; Thai bird: fierce; dried chilies: toast and rehydrate for paste; seeds and membranes hold most capsaicin|dried: years; fresh: refrigerated 1-2 weeks
IG17|Cumin|spice|warm, earthy, slightly bitter; foundation spice of Mexican, Indian, Middle Eastern, North African cooking|whole seeds: toast in dry pan until fragrant then grind (vastly superior to pre-ground); blooms in hot oil (releases flavor compounds); pairs with coriander, chili; small amount = depth; large amount = dominant|whole: 3-4 years; ground: 6 months peak
IG18|Coriander (Seed)|spice|citrusy, warm, floral; pairs with cumin across multiple cuisines; different character from fresh cilantro leaf|toast and grind whole seeds; base spice for curries, spice rubs; combines with cumin in nearly every cuisine that uses either|whole: 3-4 years; ground: 6 months
IG19|Dried Beans / Legumes|protein/starch|earthy, creamy; protein-rich; absorb surrounding flavors; inexpensive; staple worldwide|soak 8-12 hours (or quick-soak: boil 1 min, rest 1 hour); cook in unsalted water until tender (salt toughens skins if added too early — debated; acid definitely toughens); drain and season|dry: years; cooked: refrigerated 5 days; frozen months
IG20|Potato|starch vegetable|starchy, neutral, absorbs fat and seasoning; universal comfort food; fries, mash, roast, boil, soup|waxy (red, fingerling): hold shape, boiling/salad; starchy (russet): fluffy, baking/mashing/frying; all-purpose (Yukon Gold): either use; start in cold water for even cooking|cool, dark (not refrigerated — cold converts starch to sugar); away from onions
IG21|Coconut Milk|fat/liquid|rich, sweet, creamy; tropical; base for curries and desserts across South/Southeast Asia, Caribbean, West Africa|full-fat: thick cream layer + thin liquid; shake or separate depending on use; light: watered down (just use less full-fat); cream = thick part only; pairs with chili, ginger, lemongrass, lime|pantry: years (canned); refrigerated once opened: 3-5 days
IG22|Yogurt|fermented dairy|tangy, creamy; acid + fat + protein; tenderizer (marinades); cooling (raita, tzatziki); sauce base|plain, full-fat preferred for cooking; Greek = strained (thicker, higher protein); regular = pourable; don't boil (curdles — temper by adding hot liquid slowly to yogurt); acid marinates meat|refrigerated: 2-3 weeks; flavor develops over time
IG23|Fish Sauce|fermented condiment|intense umami + salt; fermented anchovy; Southeast Asian and ancient Roman (garum) foundation; adds depth without fishy flavor when used properly|a few drops = umami depth; tablespoons = primary seasoning; quality varies (first press/extract best); substitute: soy sauce + anchovy paste|pantry: years; practically immortal
IG24|Stock / Broth|liquid|savory liquid extracted from bones (stock: gelatin-rich from connective tissue) or meat (broth: lighter); foundation of soups, sauces, braises, risotto, and deglazing|stock: bones + mirepoix + aromatics, simmered 4-8 hours (chicken) or 8-24 hours (beef); gels when cold = good body; broth: quicker, meat-based; store-bought: choose low-sodium|refrigerated: 5 days; frozen: 6 months; fat cap seals and preserves
IG25|Sugar|sweetener|sweetness; caramelization; browning (Maillard with protein); fermentation food (yeast); texture (crisp, chewy, moist depending on type and amount)|white: pure sucrose, neutral; brown: white + molasses (moisture, flavor); honey: fructose + glucose, floral, sweeter than sugar by weight; jaggery/panela/piloncillo: unrefined cane, complex mineral flavor|pantry: indefinite (dry sugar); honey: indefinite
IG26|Sesame Oil (Toasted)|finishing oil|intense nutty, roasted aroma; finishing oil (not cooking oil — burns at low temperature); East Asian seasoning|a few drops = enormous flavor impact; add at very end or off heat; use as seasoning not cooking medium; raw sesame oil (untoasted) is neutral and has higher smoke point|cool, dark; 6-12 months; rancidity detectable by smell
IG27|Tahini (Sesame Paste)|paste/condiment|nutty, slightly bitter; Middle Eastern/Mediterranean foundation; emulsifies with lemon and water into creamy sauce|stir well (oil separates); thin with lemon juice + water for sauce; base for hummus, dressings, baked goods; pairs with lemon, garlic, cumin|pantry or refrigerated; 6-12 months; stir before each use
IG28|Chickpea (Garbanzo)|legume|nutty, starchy, creamy; holds shape when cooked; base for hummus, falafel, chana masala, cocido|dried: soak overnight, cook 1-2 hours; canned: convenient, rinse; aquafaba (canning liquid) whips like egg white; flour (besan/gram flour) used for batters and flatbread|dry: years; cooked: refrigerated 5 days
IG29|Lentil|legume|earthy; cooks without soaking (20-45 min depending on type); various types with different textures|red/yellow: dissolve when cooked (dal, soup); green/brown: hold shape (salads, stews); black (beluga): hold shape, elegant; French (Puy): firm, peppery|dry: years; cooked: refrigerated 5 days
IG30|Fermented Paste (Miso/Doenjang)|fermented condiment|deep umami, salty, complex; fermented soybeans (+ grain for miso); instant depth to any soup, sauce, dressing, glaze|white/shiro miso: mild, sweet; red/aka: stronger, longer fermented; miso: never boil (kills beneficial cultures; add off heat); doenjang: Korean, earthier, heartier; tablespoon in any soup/stew adds invisible depth|refrigerated: months to year+

# flavor_principles(id|name|components|origin|application)
FP1|French Mirepoix|onion + carrot + celery (2:1:1)|France|foundation of stocks, soups, braises, sauces; sautéed first; sweated (no color) or caramelized (brown); strained out or left in depending on preparation
FP2|Italian Soffritto|onion + carrot + celery (finely minced); sometimes garlic|Italy|same components as mirepoix; minced finer; sautéed in olive oil; remains in dish; base of ragù, minestrone, risotto, beans
FP3|Spanish Sofrito|onion + garlic + tomato (cooked slowly in olive oil until jammy)|Spain/Latin America|base of paella, rice dishes, beans, stews; slow cooking transforms raw ingredients into deep, sweet, concentrated base; may include peppers
FP4|Cajun/Creole Holy Trinity|onion + celery + green bell pepper (equal parts)|Louisiana, USA|replaces carrot from mirepoix with bell pepper; base of gumbo, jambalaya, étouffée, red beans; sautéed in oil or butter
FP5|Chinese / East Asian Aromatics|ginger + garlic + scallion|China, East Asia|sautéed in oil as first step of stir-fry; often with soy sauce and rice wine; ginger and garlic cut to match cooking time (minced for quick, sliced for longer)
FP6|Indian Tadka / Tarka|oil/ghee + whole spices (cumin, mustard seed, curry leaves, dried chili) + onion + ginger + garlic|India|spices bloomed in hot fat first (pop and release oils); then onion-ginger-garlic sweated; foundation of dal, curries; added at beginning or as finishing temper
FP7|Mexican Base|dried chili (ancho, guajillo, pasilla) + onion + garlic + cumin + tomato|Mexico|dried chilies toasted and rehydrated; blended with other ingredients into sauce (salsa, mole, adobo); complexity from chili variety not just heat
FP8|Thai / Southeast Asian Base|lemongrass + galangal + kaffir lime leaf + chili + shallot + garlic + shrimp paste|Thailand, Southeast Asia|pounded in mortar (curry paste); fresh aromatics; balance of hot, sour, salty, sweet in every dish; fish sauce for salt + umami; lime for acid; sugar for sweet
FP9|West African Base|onion + tomato + scotch bonnet pepper + palm oil or groundnut oil|West Africa|tomato and onion cooked until oil separates (indicates sufficient cooking); scotch bonnet for heat; palm oil for color and flavor; base for jollof rice, groundnut soup, stews
FP10|Middle Eastern / Eastern Mediterranean|onion + garlic + cumin + coriander + lemon + olive oil|Middle East/Eastern Mediterranean|spices often toasted; tahini and yogurt as sauce bases; lemon as finishing acid; parsley as herb (not garnish); warm spices (cinnamon, allspice) in savory dishes
FP11|Japanese Dashi Foundation|kombu (kelp) + katsuobushi (bonito flakes)|Japan|glutamate (kombu) + inosinate (bonito) = synergistic umami amplification (8× stronger together than separately); base of miso soup, noodle broth, simmered dishes; subtle, clean
FP12|Korean Base|gochugaru (chili flakes) + garlic + ginger + soy sauce + sesame oil + scallion|Korea|fermented ingredients (gochujang, doenjang) add depth; sesame oil as finishing; balance of spicy, sweet, savory, fermented; aggressive seasoning compared to Japanese subtlety

# techniques(id|name|definition|temperature|medium|key_principles)
TQ1|Sauté|cook quickly in small amount of fat over high heat; tossing or stirring; items cut small/thin for rapid cooking|high (200-230°C pan surface)|fat (thin layer)|pan must be hot before food enters; don't crowd pan (causes steaming); single layer; toss or stir frequently; fond develops on pan bottom; finish with sauce
TQ2|Sear|brown surface of larger piece (meat, fish) over very high heat; develop crust without cooking interior through|very high (230°C+ pan surface)|fat (thin layer)|dry surface critical (pat dry); very hot pan; don't move until crust forms (2-4 min); flip once; creates fond; searing does not "seal in juices" (myth) — it creates flavor via Maillard
TQ3|Stir-Fry|very high heat; constant motion; wok preferred; cut small and uniform; cook in batches to maintain heat|highest possible (wok hei: 300°C+)|small amount of high-smoke-point oil|prepare everything before heating wok; cook in order of density (aromatics → protein → hard veg → soft veg → sauce → garnish); small batches; wok must be screaming hot; toss constantly
TQ4|Deep Fry|submerge food in hot oil; rapid surface dehydration creates crust while interior steams|175-190°C (350-375°F); thermometer essential|oil (deep, several cm)|oil temperature critical: too low = greasy; too high = burned exterior raw interior; don't crowd (drops temperature); fry in batches; drain on wire rack (not paper towel — steam makes soggy); second fry at higher temp = crispier
TQ5|Pan Fry / Shallow Fry|cook in moderate amount of fat (halfway up item); one side at a time; flip once|medium-high (175-200°C)|fat (2-5mm depth)|items often coated (flour, breadcrumb); flip when golden; fat level between sauté and deep fry; drain well; schnitzel, cutlets, fritters
TQ6|Sweat|cook aromatics (onion, celery, garlic) gently in fat over low-medium heat without browning; softening and releasing flavors|low-medium (120-150°C)|fat (generous)|cover pot to trap steam (speeds softening); stir occasionally; translucent onion = done; no color desired; salt helps draw out moisture; foundation of soups, stews, sauces
TQ7|Caramelize (Onion)|cook onions very slowly over low heat until sugars convert to deep brown, sweet, jammy mass; 45-60+ minutes; cannot be rushed|low (120-130°C)|fat + onion moisture|patience is only technique; stir occasionally; deglaze with water if sticking (adds liquid, dissolves fond); true caramelization takes 45-60 min; recipes claiming "caramelize onions, 10 minutes" are wrong — that's sautéed onions
TQ8|Braise|combination method: sear first (dry heat → browning), then cook in liquid (wet heat → tenderizing) covered at low temperature for extended time; collagen → gelatin|sear: high; braise: 150-175°C oven or low simmer (80-95°C liquid)|liquid (1/3 to 1/2 submerged)|tough cuts become tender (collagen converts to gelatin above 70°C over time); liquid becomes sauce; aromatics and acid (wine, tomato) add depth; covered retains moisture; 2-4 hours typical; better next day (fat separates, flavors meld)
TQ9|Stew|similar to braise but food cut into pieces and fully submerged in liquid; one-pot meal|low simmer (80-95°C liquid)|liquid (fully covered)|don't boil (toughens protein at surface while interior overcooked); gentle simmer: occasional bubble; cut pieces uniform for even cooking; brown meat in batches first (fond + Maillard); thicken at end if needed
TQ10|Roast|cook in oven using dry heat; uncovered; hot air surrounds food; surface browns; interior cooks by conduction|175-230°C (350-450°F); high initial then reduce, or steady|dry air (oven)|uncovered (covering = steaming/braising); elevated on rack for air circulation; don't open oven frequently; rest after cooking; high temp for small/tender items; lower for large/tough; convection improves browning
TQ11|Bake|cook in oven using dry heat; typically for doughs, batters, casseroles; chemical and physical transformation|150-220°C (300-425°F) depending on item|dry air (oven)|oven must be preheated (temperature accuracy critical for baking); don't open door (temperature drops, items may collapse); position in oven matters (lower = more bottom heat); baking is more chemistry-dependent than roasting
TQ12|Boil / Simmer / Poach|cooking in water or water-based liquid at various temperatures|boil: 100°C (vigorous bubbles, pasta, blanching); simmer: 85-95°C (gentle bubbles, stocks, sauces, stews); poach: 70-80°C (barely trembling surface, eggs, fish, fruit)|water or liquid|boiling is violent — appropriate for pasta, blanching vegetables; simmering is gentle — most "boil" instructions actually mean simmer; poaching is gentlest — delicate items (eggs, fish) that would break apart at higher temps; altitude affects boiling point
TQ13|Steam|cook using steam from boiling water; food elevated above water; moist heat; gentle; preserves nutrients and color|100°C (steam)|steam|food not submerged; steamer basket, bamboo steamer, or improvised; lid essential (traps steam); timing critical (easy to overcook); vegetables retain color and nutrients better than boiling; dumplings, fish, vegetables
TQ14|Blanch and Shock|brief immersion in boiling water then immediate transfer to ice water; partially cooks; sets color; loosens skins|boil: 100°C → ice water: 0°C|water → ice water|timing: 30 seconds to 3 minutes depending on item; preserves vibrant color (chlorophyll set); stops cooking instantly (shock); loosens tomato and peach skins; removes raw edge from vegetables before stir-fry or salad; prep-ahead technique
TQ15|Grill / Char|cook over direct radiant heat from below (grill) or above (broiler/salamander); very high heat; char marks; smoky flavor|260°C+ (500°F+) at grate surface|radiant heat + fat/drippings|preheat grill thoroughly; clean and oil grate; don't move food until it releases naturally (crust formed); direct heat = searing/thin items; indirect heat = thicker items (move away from direct after searing); flare-ups from dripping fat = bitter
TQ16|Smoke|exposing food to wood smoke at various temperatures; cold smoking (below 30°C: flavor only) or hot smoking (95-135°C: cooks and flavors)|cold: <30°C; hot: 95-135°C|smoke + heat|wood type determines flavor (mesquite: strong; apple/cherry: mild, sweet; hickory: medium, classic; oak: medium); low and slow; moisture in smoker prevents drying; salt/brine first for flavor and safety (cold smoke especially)
TQ17|Deglaze|adding liquid to hot pan containing fond (browned bits); liquid dissolves fond incorporating flavors into sauce|hot pan (after searing/sautéing)|wine, stock, vinegar, water|remove excess fat first if desired; add liquid to hot pan (will steam vigorously); scrape fond with wooden spoon; reduce liquid by half or more; mount with butter or cream for finish; wine → stock → reduce → finish is classic pan sauce sequence
TQ18|Emulsify (Technique)|mechanically or chemically combining oil and water into stable mixture; gradual oil addition while whisking/blending; emulsifier stabilizes|room temp to warm (depending on preparation)|oil + water-phase + emulsifier|add oil very slowly at first (teaspoon at a time for mayonnaise); emulsifier must be present (egg yolk, mustard, garlic); broken emulsion: start new with tiny amount of water/mustard, drizzle broken mixture in slowly; temperature shock breaks emulsion
TQ19|Toast (Spices/Nuts/Seeds)|dry heat in pan or oven to develop flavor; volatile oils released; color deepens; flavor intensifies|medium heat; dry pan (no oil)|dry heat|stir or shake constantly (burn quickly and unevenly); remove from hot pan immediately when fragrant (carryover cooking continues); toast whole spices then grind (superior to pre-ground); nuts: watch carefully, they go from perfect to burned in seconds
TQ20|Bloom (Spices in Fat)|adding ground or whole spices to hot oil or butter; fat-soluble flavor compounds dissolve into fat; flavor distributes through dish|medium heat|fat|spices added to hot fat before other ingredients (tadka/tarka method); 30-60 seconds until fragrant; bloomed spices have 2-3× more flavor impact than spices added dry; foundation of Indian, Mexican, Ethiopian, many other cuisines
TQ21|Marinate|soaking food in seasoned liquid (acid + oil + aromatics) to flavor and optionally tenderize|room temp or refrigerated|acid + oil + aromatics|acid marinades (citrus, vinegar) tenderize surface but toughen if too long (>2 hours for delicate protein); enzymatic marinades (pineapple, papaya, kiwi) truly tenderize; yogurt marinades: acid + fat + tenderize gently; salt in marinade = most effective penetrating flavor; flavor penetration is shallow (~3-6mm)
TQ22|Brine|soaking protein in salt water solution (5-10% salt by weight of water); salt and water penetrate via osmosis and diffusion; seasons interior; increases moisture retention|cold (refrigerated)|salt water solution|equilibrium brine: 5-10% salt, soak until salt equalizes through meat (24-72 hrs depending on thickness); wet brine: shorter, more dilute; dry brine (salting): salt rubbed directly on surface, uncovered, refrigerated 1-24 hrs; dry brine simpler, less mess, similar results

PV1|Salt Curing (Dry)|meat or fish, salt (2.5-3% of meat weight for short cure; 10-20% for preservation), sugar (optional, balances salt), aromatics|weigh meat; calculate salt at 2.5-3% of weight; mix salt + sugar + any spices; coat all surfaces; place in container; refrigerate; cure time: 1 day per 1cm thickness; rinse surface; pat dry; use or continue to air-dry|osmosis draws moisture from cells; reduced water activity inhibits microbial growth; salt denatures surface proteins creating firm texture; concentration-dependent: light cure (flavoring) vs heavy cure (preservation)|light cure (2.5-3%): use within 1-2 weeks refrigerated; heavy cure (10%+): weeks to months; fully dried (bresaola, biltong): months at room temperature
PV2|Lacto-Fermentation|vegetables 1 kg (cabbage, cucumbers, carrots, radish), salt 20g (2% of vegetable weight), water (if needed)|chop or shred vegetables; toss with salt; pack tightly into jar, pressing until brine rises above vegetables (for cabbage/sauerkraut: salt and massage releases liquid; for whole vegetables: add 2-3% salt brine to cover); weight vegetables below brine surface; cover loosely (gases must escape); ferment at room temp 3-7 days (taste daily); refrigerate when desired sourness reached|salt suppresses harmful bacteria while favoring Lactobacillus; lactic acid produced lowers pH → preserves; submerged = anaerobic environment (prevents mold); salt percentage critical: too little = spoilage; too much = no fermentation|refrigerated: months; continues fermenting slowly in fridge; sauerkraut, kimchi, kosher dill pickles, curtido, preserved lemons all use this method
PV3|Vinegar Pickling (Quick)|vegetables, vinegar 250ml, water 250ml, salt 15g, sugar 30g, optional spices (mustard seed, dill, peppercorn, chili)|bring vinegar + water + salt + sugar + spices to boil; pack vegetables in clean jar; pour hot brine over; cool; refrigerate|acetic acid in vinegar lowers pH below 4.6 (inhibits botulism and most spoilage organisms); not fermented (vinegar provides acid directly); quick and reliable|refrigerated: 2-3 months; not shelf-stable without proper canning (water bath processing for shelf stability)
PV4|Jam / Fruit Preserve|fruit 1 kg (any), sugar 600-800g (60-80% of fruit weight), lemon juice 30ml, pectin (if fruit is low-pectin)|cook fruit with sugar over medium heat until sugar dissolves; bring to rolling boil; add pectin if using; boil until gel point reached (105°C / 220°F; or plate test: drop on cold plate, push with finger — wrinkles = done); add lemon juice; pour into sterilized jars; seal|high sugar concentration reduces water activity; acid (lemon) aids gel formation and inhibits spoilage; pectin creates gel network; boiling to 105°C concentrates to ~65% sugar (preservation threshold); proper canning (water bath 10 min) for shelf stability|sealed properly: 1 year shelf-stable; opened: refrigerated 3-4 weeks; pectin content varies by fruit: apple, citrus, quince = high; strawberry, peach, cherry = low (add pectin or high-pectin fruit)
PV5|Confit|meat (traditionally duck legs) or garlic or vegetables, salt, fat (duck fat, olive oil, lard)|salt meat with 2.5% salt + aromatics; cure refrigerated 24-48 hours; rinse; submerge in fat; cook in low oven 135°C for 2-4 hours until tender (falls off bone for duck); cool in fat; store submerged in fat|slow cooking in fat tenderizes; fat seal excludes air (anaerobic → preservation); salt cure seasons and begins preservation; stored under fat in refrigerator or cool cellar; garlic confit: whole cloves slow-cooked in olive oil until soft and sweet|submerged under fat, refrigerated: 3-6 months (meat); garlic confit in oil: refrigerate and use within 2-3 weeks (botulism risk with garlic in oil at room temperature — always refrigerate)

# failure_modes(id|topic|mode|cause|consequence|prevention)
FM1|heat|burning garlic|too high heat; added too early (before onion); left unattended|bitter, acrid flavor that permeates entire dish; cannot be rescued; must restart|add garlic after onion has softened; medium heat; stir frequently; garlic needs only 30-60 seconds; if garlic begins to darken beyond golden, add next ingredient immediately (liquid, tomato) to stop cooking
FM2|heat|steaming instead of searing|wet protein surface; crowded pan; pan not hot enough; too much oil|gray, pallid meat with no Maillard flavor; boiled texture; no fond for sauce|pat protein completely dry; screaming hot pan; single layer with space between pieces; cook in batches; minimal oil; don't move until crust forms
FM3|seasoning|underseasoning|fear of salt; adding salt only at end; not tasting throughout cooking|flat, dull food that tastes of nothing despite good ingredients and technique|season at every stage (onion, meat, sauce, finishing); taste constantly; season earlier rather than later (allows penetration); final adjustment at plating; salt brings out existing flavors — it doesn't add saltiness until excess
FM4|sauce|broken emulsion|temperature too high or too low; adding oil too fast; ingredients at different temperatures|separated, greasy, curdled sauce or dressing; mayonnaise breaks; beurre blanc splits|add oil slowly (especially at start); consistent temperature; start with small amount of water/acid/mustard and rebuild if broken; don't boil butter-mounted sauces; temper ingredients to similar temperatures
FM5|baking|dense bread/cake|under-kneaded (bread); over-mixed (cake/muffins); dead yeast; insufficient fermentation time; oven too low|heavy, gummy, flat result; no rise; tight crumb|test yeast (should foam in warm water with sugar in 5-10 min); knead bread sufficiently (windowpane test); mix cake/muffin batter only until just combined (gluten development = tough); correct oven temperature (use oven thermometer)
FM6|vegetable|overcooked vegetables|boiling too long; steaming too long; roasting at too low temperature; not blanching/shocking|mushy, dull-colored, nutrient-depleted; gray-green instead of vibrant green; falling apart|blanch and shock green vegetables; roast at high heat; test frequently (knife should enter with slight resistance); cook less than you think (carryover continues)
FM7|rice|mushy/sticky rice|too much water; stirring during cooking; not resting after cooking; using wrong rice type for intended result|gummy, starchy, clumped; porridge when you wanted fluffy individual grains|correct ratio for rice type; rinse until clear; do not lift lid during cooking; rest 10 min after heat off; do not stir (breaks grains, releases starch); use long grain for fluffy
FM8|texture|crowded pan|putting too much food in pan at once; especially relevant for searing, sautéing, roasting|food steams instead of browns; temperature drops dramatically; water released from food pools instead of evaporating; limp, gray results|cook in batches; single layer; space between pieces; better to cook in 3 batches with excellent results than 1 batch with mediocre; patience is a technique
FM9|flavor|acid missing|no acid component in finished dish; richness without brightness; heaviness without lift|food tastes heavy, flat, one-dimensional despite good seasoning; something feels missing but isn't salt|finish almost every savory dish with acid (lemon squeeze, vinegar dash, tomato); taste before and after acid addition (revelation); acid brightens, lifts, and balances richness; the most common "missing something" in home cooking

# distinctions(id|side_a|side_b|key_asymmetry)
DI1|Sear / Brown|Cook Through|searing: very high heat, short time, develops Maillard crust on surface, interior may be raw; cooking through: moderate heat, longer time, ensures internal doneness; often combined: sear first then gentle heat to finish; searing is for flavor (not "sealing in juices" — disproven myth)
DI2|Sweat|Caramelize|sweat: low heat, no color, softening and releasing moisture/flavor gently (translucent onion); caramelize: low heat but long time, developing brown color and deep sweetness (45-60 min for onions); completely different flavor profiles from same ingredient and similar temperature
DI3|Simmer|Boil|simmer: gentle bubbles occasionally breaking surface (85-95°C); gentle cooking for most applications (stock, sauce, braise, stew); boil: vigorous, rolling bubbles (100°C); aggressive, appropriate for pasta, blanching, reducing; most recipes saying "boil" actually mean "simmer"; hard boiling toughens protein surfaces, agitates delicate foods, emulsifies fat into stocks making them cloudy
DI4|Rest (Before Salt)|Rest (After Cook)|resting before cooking: salt → rest → dry surface (better sear); salt immediately before = surface moisture drawn out but not re-absorbed (worst timing); salt 40+ min before = moisture drawn out, salt dissolves, re-absorbed with seasoning; resting after cooking: internal temperature equalizes, juices redistribute from center to edge, cutting too soon = juice loss
DI5|Mise en Place|Cooking|mise en place: all preparation before heat applied; measuring, cutting, organizing; takes 70-80% of total kitchen time; cooking: actual heat application; typically 20-30% of total time; most home cooking failures come from inadequate mise en place (scrambling for ingredients while food burns)
DI6|Recipe|Technique|recipe: specific list of ingredients and steps for one dish; following recipes = dependency (can only cook what you've memorized); technique: transferable skill applicable across many recipes; understanding technique = independence (can improvise, substitute, create); braising technique works for any cuisine; memorizing one braise recipe gives you one dish
DI7|Seasoning|Flavoring|seasoning: enhancing existing flavors (salt, acid, pepper); makes food taste more like itself; applied throughout cooking; flavoring: adding new flavor (spices, herbs, aromatics, sauces); changes what food tastes like; layered during cooking; seasoning is universal and essential; flavoring is cultural and variable
DI8|Fresh Herb|Dried Herb|fresh: added at end (volatile oils dissipate with heat); bright, immediate flavor; garnish; larger quantity needed; dried: added early (needs time to rehydrate and release flavor into liquid); concentrated (typically 1/3 quantity of fresh); some herbs better dried (oregano, thyme); some only good fresh (basil, cilantro, parsley, mint, dill)
DI9|Fond|Burnt Bits|fond: brown, caramelized deposits stuck to pan bottom; dissolved by deglazing; full of Maillard flavor; deepens sauces; develops at proper searing temperature; burnt bits: black, carbonized deposits from excessive heat; bitter, acrid; cannot be rescued; indicates heat was too high or pan was neglected; fond = gold; burnt bits = trash
DI10|Emulsion|Suspension|emulsion: stable mixture of oil and water held together by emulsifier (lecithin, mustard, starch); can persist for hours to indefinitely (mayonnaise); requires technique to form; suspension: temporary dispersion that separates over time (basic vinaigrette without mustard); must be re-mixed before use; no emulsifier present
