# concepts(id|name|definition|category)
CO1|Kinesthesia|awareness of body position and movement in space without visual input; mediated by proprioceptors|sensation
CO2|Proprioception|sensory input from mechanoreceptors in muscles, tendons, joints, and fascia reporting position, force, and velocity|sensation
CO3|Vestibular Sense|inner ear detection of head position, angular velocity, and linear acceleration via semicircular canals and otolith organs|sensation
CO4|Motor Unit|single motor neuron + all muscle fibers it innervates; smallest controllable unit of force production|neuromuscular
CO5|Recruitment|progressive activation of motor units by size (Henneman size principle); small (slow-twitch) first, large (fast-twitch) last|neuromuscular
CO6|Rate Coding|modulating force by changing motor neuron firing frequency; higher frequency = more force per motor unit|neuromuscular
CO7|Motor Engram|stored neural pattern for a practiced movement; encoded in motor cortex, cerebellum, basal ganglia|neuromuscular
CO8|Neuromuscular Junction|synapse between motor neuron terminal and muscle fiber; acetylcholine crosses gap → depolarization → contraction|neuromuscular
CO9|Muscle Spindle|intrafusal fiber sensing muscle length and rate of length change; drives stretch reflex|proprioceptor
CO10|Golgi Tendon Organ (GTO)|receptor in musculotendinous junction sensing tension; triggers autogenic inhibition at high loads|proprioceptor
CO11|Joint Mechanoreceptor|Ruffini endings, Pacinian corpuscles in joint capsule sensing pressure, vibration, position|proprioceptor
CO12|Fascicle|bundle of muscle fibers within a muscle, surrounded by perimysium|structure
CO13|Sarcomere|functional contractile unit of muscle; Z-line to Z-line; contains actin and myosin filaments|structure
CO14|Sliding Filament Theory|myosin heads bind actin, pull thin filaments toward M-line, shortening sarcomere; powered by ATP hydrolysis|mechanism
CO15|Cross-Bridge Cycle|attach → pull → detach → reset; myosin binds actin (rigor), power stroke (ADP release), detach (ATP bind), cock (ATP hydrolysis)|mechanism
CO16|Excitation-Contraction Coupling|action potential → T-tubule → Ca²⁺ release from sarcoplasmic reticulum → troponin conformational change → actin binding sites exposed|mechanism
CO17|All-or-None Principle|individual muscle fiber contracts maximally or not at all; force modulation via recruitment and rate coding, not partial fiber contraction|principle
CO18|Length-Tension Relationship|muscle produces maximal force at intermediate sarcomere length (~2.0-2.25 μm); too short = actin overlap; too long = insufficient cross-bridges|principle
CO19|Force-Velocity Relationship|concentric: force decreases as velocity increases (Hill's equation); eccentric: force increases above isometric maximum|principle
CO20|Stretch-Shortening Cycle (SSC)|eccentric pre-stretch followed immediately by concentric contraction; stores elastic energy + triggers stretch reflex; enhances power output|mechanism
CO21|Elastic Energy Storage|tendon and titin deformation during eccentric phase stores potential energy; released during concentric phase; up to 35% of total work in running|mechanism
CO22|Reciprocal Inhibition|activation of agonist simultaneously inhibits antagonist via Ia interneurons; enables smooth joint movement|reflex
CO23|Autogenic Inhibition|GTO-mediated reflex reducing force in contracting muscle when tension exceeds threshold; protective mechanism|reflex
CO24|Stretch Reflex (Myotatic)|muscle spindle detects rapid stretch → monosynaptic reflex arc → same muscle contracts; latency ~30ms|reflex
CO25|Righting Reflex|automatic postural adjustment to maintain upright position; integrates vestibular, visual, proprioceptive input|reflex
CO26|Wolff's Law|bone adapts to loads placed upon it; increased loading → increased density and structural strength; decreased loading → resorption|adaptation
CO27|Davis's Law|soft tissue remodels along lines of stress; aligned collagen deposition in tendons under appropriate load|adaptation
CO28|SAID Principle (Specific Adaptation to Imposed Demands)|body adapts specifically to the type of demand placed on it; training must match desired adaptation|principle
CO29|General Adaptation Syndrome (GAS)|Selye's alarm → resistance → exhaustion model; training is controlled stress; recovery enables supercompensation|principle
CO30|Supercompensation|fitness rebounds above baseline after recovery from training stress; window for next stimulus|principle
CO31|Progressive Overload|systematic increase in training stress (load, volume, density, complexity) over time to drive continued adaptation|principle
CO32|Reversibility|adaptations decay when stimulus removed; cardiovascular fitness declines faster (~2 weeks) than strength (~3-4 weeks)|principle
CO33|Specificity|transfer of training is highest when training closely mimics target movement in velocity, load, range, and energy system|principle
CO34|Bilateral Deficit|combined force of both limbs contracting simultaneously < sum of each limb contracting independently; ~5-25% deficit|phenomenon
CO35|Rate of Force Development (RFD)|slope of force-time curve; how quickly force can be produced; critical for explosive movements; measured in N/s|metric
CO36|Ground Reaction Force (GRF)|Newton's third law applied to locomotion; force exerted by ground on body equals force body exerts on ground|force
CO37|Center of Mass (COM)|point representing average position of total body mass; location shifts with body configuration|biomechanics
CO38|Center of Pressure (COP)|point on support surface where total ground reaction force vector acts; moves dynamically during stance|biomechanics
CO39|Base of Support (BOS)|area enclosed by contact points with support surface; wider BOS = greater static stability|biomechanics
CO40|Moment of Inertia|resistance to angular acceleration; I = Σmr²; depends on mass distribution relative to axis of rotation|biomechanics
CO41|Angular Momentum|L = Iω; product of moment of inertia and angular velocity; conserved in flight (no external torques)|biomechanics
CO42|Torque (Moment)|force × perpendicular distance from axis of rotation; produces angular acceleration; τ = r × F|biomechanics
CO43|Lever System|rigid body (bone) rotating about fulcrum (joint) with effort (muscle) and resistance (load); 3 classes|biomechanics
CO44|Mechanical Advantage|ratio of output force to input force; MA = effort arm / resistance arm; most joints have MA < 1 (speed advantage)|biomechanics
CO45|Kinetic Chain|sequential activation of body segments transferring force from proximal to distal; open vs closed|biomechanics
CO46|Proximal-to-Distal Sequencing|force generation begins at large proximal segments (hips/trunk) and transfers through progressively smaller distal segments|biomechanics
CO47|Stiffness (musculotendinous)|resistance to deformation; higher stiffness = faster force transmission; trained via plyometrics and heavy loading|property
CO48|Compliance|inverse of stiffness; ability to deform under load; necessary for shock absorption|property
CO49|Viscoelasticity|tissue exhibits both viscous (rate-dependent) and elastic (rate-independent) behavior; creep and stress relaxation|property
CO50|Creep|progressive deformation under constant load over time; relevant to sustained stretching and prolonged postures|property
CO51|Stress Relaxation|decrease in internal stress under constant deformation over time; tissue "gives" during sustained stretch|property
CO52|Thixotropy|stiffness decreases with movement and increases with immobility; "warming up" reduces tissue resistance|property
CO53|Fascia|connective tissue network enveloping muscles, organs, nerves; transmits force; contains proprioceptors; superficial and deep layers|structure
CO54|Tendon|dense connective tissue connecting muscle to bone; transmits contractile force; high tensile strength; low vascularity; collagen type I dominant|structure
CO55|Ligament|dense connective tissue connecting bone to bone; stabilizes joints; limited elasticity; collagen type I dominant with some elastin|structure
CO56|Cartilage (Articular)|avascular connective tissue covering joint surfaces; distributes load; reduces friction (coefficient ~0.001); limited regeneration|structure
CO57|Intervertebral Disc|fibrocartilage between vertebrae; nucleus pulposus (gel core) + annulus fibrosus (concentric rings); distributes axial load|structure
CO58|Pennation Angle|angle of muscle fibers relative to tendon direction; higher pennation = more fibers packed = more force; less shortening velocity|structure
CO59|Anatomical Cross-Sectional Area (ACSA)|cross-section perpendicular to muscle belly long axis; correlates with force in parallel-fibered muscles|metric
CO60|Physiological Cross-Sectional Area (PCSA)|total area perpendicular to all fibers; accounts for pennation; better predictor of maximum force than ACSA|metric
CO61|Motor Learning|relatively permanent change in motor behavior resulting from practice or experience; acquisition → retention → transfer|process
CO62|Stages of Motor Learning (Fitts & Posner)|cognitive → associative → autonomous; attention demand decreases; consistency increases; error detection internalizes|model
CO63|Degrees of Freedom Problem (Bernstein)|musculoskeletal system has more DOF than needed for any task; motor control must constrain redundant DOF; solved by synergies|problem
CO64|Synergy (Motor)|coordinated multi-muscle, multi-joint pattern reducing effective DOF; emerges through practice; flexibly adapts to perturbation|mechanism
CO65|Feed-Forward Control|pre-planned motor commands issued before sensory feedback available; based on internal model; anticipatory postural adjustments|control
CO66|Feedback Control|movement corrections based on sensory input during execution; slower than feed-forward; visual ~200ms, proprioceptive ~80ms|control
CO67|Internal Model|neural representation of body-environment dynamics; forward model predicts movement outcomes; inverse model computes needed commands|control
CO68|Schema Theory (Schmidt)|generalized motor program stores invariant features (relative timing, relative force); parameters (absolute timing, absolute force, effector) specified per execution|theory
CO69|Dynamical Systems Theory|movement emerges from interaction of organism, task, and environment constraints; self-organization; attractors and phase transitions|theory
CO70|Affordance|perceived action possibility offered by environment relative to actor's capabilities; directly perceived, not computed|perception
CO71|Anticipation|predicting future events/positions based on contextual cues; expert advantage = earlier and more accurate prediction from fewer cues|skill
CO72|Quiet Eye|final fixation on target before movement initiation; longer duration correlates with better accuracy in aiming tasks; trainable|skill
CO73|Variability (motor)|fluctuation in movement execution across repetitions; optimal variability enables adaptability; excessive = noise; too little = rigidity|property

# biomechanical_principles(id|name|definition|application|formula)
BP1|Newton's First Law (Inertia)|body remains at rest or constant velocity unless acted upon by net external force|maintaining momentum in sprinting; overcoming inertia in starts|F_net = 0 → Δv = 0
BP2|Newton's Second Law|net force = mass × acceleration; governs all acceleration in sport|sprint acceleration; force production in jumping|F = ma
BP3|Newton's Third Law|every action has equal and opposite reaction; basis of locomotion|GRF in running; push-off forces; swimming propulsion|F_AB = -F_BA
BP4|Impulse-Momentum|impulse (F×t) equals change in momentum; longer force application = greater velocity change|jumping: increase GRF or contact time; landing: extend time to reduce force|J = FΔt = mΔv
BP5|Work-Energy Theorem|net work done = change in kinetic energy|sprinting: work by muscles converts to kinetic energy|W = ΔKE = ½mv₂² - ½mv₁²
BP6|Conservation of Angular Momentum|angular momentum constant when no external torque; change shape to change spin rate|figure skating spin: tuck arms → decrease I → increase ω; diving tuck|L = Iω = constant (no external torque)
BP7|Conservation of Energy|total mechanical energy (KE + PE) constant in absence of non-conservative forces; dissipated by friction/deformation in real systems|pole vault: KE → elastic PE → gravitational PE|KE + PE = constant (ideal)
BP8|Projectile Motion|once airborne, only gravity acts (ignoring air resistance); parabolic trajectory determined by launch velocity, angle, height|jumping, throwing; optimal angle ~42-45° from ground level|range = v²sin(2θ)/g; height = v²sin²(θ)/2g
BP9|Centripetal Force|force directed toward center of circular path; required for curved movement|running curves; discus rotation; cycling velodrome|F_c = mv²/r
BP10|Torque and Rotational Equilibrium|net torque = 0 for rotational equilibrium; joint torque = muscle force × moment arm|joint loading analysis; determining muscle force requirements|Στ = 0 (equilibrium); τ = Fd sin(θ)
BP11|Bernoulli's Principle|faster fluid flow = lower pressure; explains aerodynamic lift and curve|baseball curve, soccer bend, javelin flight, cycling drafting|P₁ + ½ρv₁² + ρgh₁ = P₂ + ½ρv₂² + ρgh₂
BP12|Coefficient of Friction|ratio of friction force to normal force; static > kinetic; governs traction|shoe-surface interaction; grip; sliding vs. planting|f = μN; μ_s > μ_k
BP13|Coefficient of Restitution|ratio of relative velocity after to before collision; 1 = perfectly elastic; 0 = perfectly inelastic|ball bounce; implement impact; surface compliance|e = v_after / v_before (separation/approach)
BP14|Buoyancy (Archimedes)|upward force = weight of displaced fluid; governs swimming body position|body composition affects floating position; lean sinks, fat floats|F_b = ρ_fluid × V_displaced × g
BP15|Drag Force|resistance from fluid medium; proportional to v² at athletic speeds|sprinting: ~8-16% energy at top speed; cycling: dominant resistance >30 km/h|F_d = ½ρv²C_dA

# distinctions(id|side_a|side_b|key_asymmetry)
DI1|Concentric Contraction|Eccentric Contraction|concentric: muscle shortens under load; force < capacity at high velocity; less damage; eccentric: muscle lengthens under load; can produce 20-50% more force than concentric; greater mechanical damage and remodeling stimulus
