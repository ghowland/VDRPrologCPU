# domains(id|name|definition)
DM1|site work|grading, excavation, drainage, soil preparation, setback compliance, utility location, erosion control; everything before foundation
DM2|foundation|structural base transferring building loads to earth; types determined by soil bearing capacity, frost depth, water table, and building loads
DM3|structural framing|skeleton of building: walls, floors, roof structure; carries gravity loads (dead + live), lateral loads (wind, seismic), and transfers to foundation
DM4|building envelope|weather barrier separating conditioned interior from exterior: roof, walls, windows, doors; controls water, air, vapor, and thermal transfer
DM5|mechanical (HVAC)|heating, ventilation, and air conditioning; maintains indoor air quality and thermal comfort; sized to building load
DM6|plumbing|supply (potable water in), drain-waste-vent (DWV: wastewater out), fixtures, water heating; pressure + gravity systems
DM7|electrical|power distribution, lighting, devices, grounding, overcurrent protection; life-safety (smoke/CO detection)
DM8|interior finishes|drywall, paint, trim, flooring, cabinets, countertops; non-structural but define livable space
DM9|moisture management|integrated system controlling liquid water, water vapor, and condensation through drainage planes, vapor barriers/retarders, ventilation, and flashing
DM10|energy performance|thermal envelope, air sealing, mechanical efficiency, renewable integration; code compliance (IECC) and beyond-code targets (Passive House, Net Zero)

# concepts(id|name|definition|category)
BC1|load path|continuous transfer of loads from point of application to foundation; every structural connection must be part of an unbroken chain: roof → walls → floors → foundation → soil; failure at any link = structural failure|structural
BC2|continuous load path (uplift)|specific chain resisting wind uplift: roof sheathing (nailed to rafters/trusses) → hurricane tie (rafter to plate) → stud-to-plate connection → holdown (stud to foundation) → anchor bolt → foundation; every link engineered for design wind|structural
BC3|building envelope|conceptual boundary between conditioned (interior) and unconditioned (exterior) space; composed of thermal barrier (insulation), air barrier, water barrier (WRB), and vapor control; all four must be continuous and connected|envelope
BC4|thermal bridging|heat transfer through structural members (studs, rafters, concrete) that bypasses insulation; studs at R-4 in R-19 wall reduce effective wall R to ~R-14; eliminated by continuous exterior insulation (CI) which covers studs|envelope
BC5|stack effect|warm air rises and exits at top of building → draws cold air in at bottom; pressure differential proportional to height × temperature difference; drives air leakage and moisture transport; worse in tall buildings and cold climates|building science
BC6|dew point in wall assembly|temperature at which water vapor condenses; if dew point falls within wall assembly (on cold surface), condensation occurs → moisture → mold/rot; exterior CI keeps sheathing above dew point (warm side of foam stays warm); must calculate for climate|building science
BC7|hygrothermal analysis|analysis of simultaneous heat and moisture transport through building assemblies; determines where condensation occurs, whether assembly dries, and long-term moisture performance; WUFI software is standard tool; essential for unusual assemblies or cold climates|building science
BC8|right-sizing (HVAC)|selecting HVAC equipment capacity to match building heating and cooling loads; Manual J (ACCA): room-by-room load calculation → total load → equipment selection; oversized equipment: short-cycles (on/off frequently → poor dehumidification, wear, discomfort); undersized: can't meet load on design day|mechanical
BC9|continuous insulation (CI)|insulation that is continuous across all structural members, without thermal bridging; exterior rigid foam or mineral wool board is most common; code-required in many climate zones; inch-for-inch more effective than cavity insulation because no thermal bridging|envelope
BC10|flashing principle|water drains by gravity; every horizontal or angled joint in the building envelope must direct water outward via overlapping materials arranged shingle-style (upper over lower); flashing = thin impervious material (metal or membrane) bridging joints; most water intrusion failures are flashing failures|moisture
BC11|blower door test|diagnostic test measuring whole-building air leakage; calibrated fan depressurizes building to −50 Pa; airflow through fan = total air leakage at 50 Pa; reported as CFM50 or ACH50; identifies leakiness; combined with smoke or infrared camera to locate specific leaks|testing
BC12|Manual J load calculation|engineering procedure (ACCA Manual J) to determine heating and cooling loads of a building based on: orientation, insulation levels, air leakage rate, window area/type/orientation, occupancy, climate data, and internal gains; output: peak heating BTU/h and peak cooling BTU/h → used to select equipment|mechanical
BC13|IRC (International Residential Code)|model building code for one- and two-family dwellings; covers structural, mechanical, plumbing, electrical, energy; adopted by most US jurisdictions (often with local amendments); companion codes: IBC (commercial), IECC (energy), NEC (electrical, NFPA 70)|code
BC14|prescriptive vs performance path|code compliance methods: prescriptive: follow specific requirements (R-values, material specifications, methods) from code tables; performance: demonstrate equivalent or better performance through analysis (energy modeling, engineering calculation); prescriptive is simpler; performance allows innovation and trade-offs|code
BC15|vapor drive|direction of water vapor migration through assemblies: from high vapor pressure (warm, humid) to low vapor pressure (cold, dry); in winter (cold climates): vapor drives outward (from heated interior to cold exterior); in summer (hot-humid climates): vapor drives inward (from hot humid exterior to cooled interior); assembly must accommodate dominant vapor drive direction|building science
