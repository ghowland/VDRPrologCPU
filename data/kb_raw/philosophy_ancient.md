# concepts(id|name|definition|category)
CO1|Logos|rational principle governing cosmos; speech, reason, cosmic order; Heraclitus: underlying unity of change; Stoics: divine rational fire pervading all|metaphysics
CO2|Nous (Mind/Intellect)|pure intellective faculty; Anaxagoras: cosmic mind ordering matter; Aristotle: active intellect actualizing knowledge; Plotinus: second hypostasis|metaphysics
CO3|Arche|first principle or origin of all things; primary question of Presocratics; each proposed different arche|metaphysics
CO4|Physis (Nature)|intrinsic nature of things; natural world as object of inquiry; contrasted with nomos (convention)|metaphysics
CO5|Kosmos|ordered universe; Pythagoras credited with first use; implies rational structure amenable to investigation|metaphysics
CO6|Being (To On)|that which exists; Parmenides: being is one, unchanging, eternal; central problem: relation of being to becoming|metaphysics
CO7|Becoming (Genesis)|process of change; Heraclitus: fundamental reality is flux; Plato: realm of sensible appearances, inferior to Being|metaphysics
CO8|Substance (Ousia)|primary being; Aristotle: that which underlies properties; concrete individual (primary) or species/genus (secondary)|metaphysics
CO9|Form (Eidos/Morphe)|Plato: eternal intelligible archetype (Idea/Form) existing independently; Aristotle: structural principle inseparable from matter; what makes a thing what it is|metaphysics
CO10|Matter (Hyle)|Aristotle: pure potentiality; substrate receiving form; prime matter has no properties of its own|metaphysics
CO11|Hylomorphism|Aristotle's doctrine: every physical substance is composite of form (morphe) and matter (hyle)|metaphysics
CO12|Potentiality and Actuality (Dynamis/Energeia)|Aristotle: potentiality = capacity to become; actuality = fulfillment of capacity; acorn/oak; explains change without Parmenidean paradox|metaphysics
CO13|The One (To Hen)|Plotinus: supreme principle beyond being and thought; source of all emanation; utterly simple; knowable only via negation or mystical union|metaphysics
CO14|Emanation|Plotinus: reality flows necessarily from The One → Nous → Soul → Matter; not creation by will but overflow of perfection|metaphysics
CO15|Participation (Methexis)|Plato: sensible things participate in or imitate Forms; explains resemblance between particular and universal|metaphysics
CO16|Universals|general properties (redness, humanity); realists: exist independently; nominalists: names only; conceptualists: mental constructs|metaphysics
CO17|Telos (Final Cause/Purpose)|end toward which a thing naturally tends; Aristotle: nature does nothing in vain; intrinsic purposiveness|metaphysics
CO18|Four Causes|Aristotle: material (stuff), formal (structure), efficient (agent), final (purpose); complete explanation requires all four|metaphysics
CO19|Unmoved Mover|Aristotle: first cause of all motion; pure actuality (energeia); eternal; thinks itself; attracts as object of desire, not by pushing|metaphysics
CO20|Atomism|Democritus/Leucippus: reality = indivisible atoms (atoma) moving in void; all properties from atomic arrangement; Epicurus adds swerve (clinamen)|metaphysics
CO21|Void (Kenon)|empty space; atomists: necessary for motion; Aristotle denies void; Stoics: exists outside cosmos only|metaphysics
CO22|Eternal Return|Stoics: cosmos cyclically destroyed by fire (ekpyrosis) and regenerated identically; Nietzsche later reinterprets|metaphysics
CO23|Dao (The Way)|central concept in Chinese philosophy; Laozi: nameless source of all things; natural order; wu wei follows Dao; Confucius: moral way of conduct|metaphysics
CO24|Li (Principle/Pattern)|Neo-Confucianism (Zhu Xi): rational principle inherent in all things; analogous to form; li + qi = concrete thing|metaphysics
CO25|Qi (Vital Force/Material Force)|Chinese philosophy: dynamic material-energetic substrate; condensed = matter; dispersed = energy; pervades all|metaphysics
CO26|Brahman|Hindu philosophy: ultimate reality; Advaita Vedanta (Shankara): nirguna (without qualities), identical with Atman; Vishishtadvaita (Ramanuja): saguna (with qualities)|metaphysics
CO27|Atman|Hindu philosophy: true self; Upanishads: identical with Brahman (tat tvam asi); Buddhists deny permanent self (anatman)|metaphysics
CO28|Sunyata (Emptiness)|Nagarjuna/Madhyamaka Buddhism: all dharmas lack inherent self-existence (svabhava); emptiness is itself empty; not nihilism but dependent origination|metaphysics
CO29|Dependent Origination (Pratityasamutpada)|Buddhist doctrine: all phenomena arise in dependence on causes and conditions; nothing exists independently; 12 links of cyclic existence|metaphysics
CO30|Eudaimonia (Flourishing)|Aristotle: highest human good; activity of soul in accordance with virtue (arete) over complete life; not mere pleasure or wealth|ethics
CO31|Arete (Virtue/Excellence)|functional excellence; Socrates: virtue is knowledge; Aristotle: habit (hexis) of choosing the mean; cardinal virtues: prudence, justice, courage, temperance|ethics
CO32|Phronesis (Practical Wisdom)|Aristotle: intellectual virtue of deliberating well about what is good for human life; required for all moral virtues; cannot be taught by rules alone|ethics
CO33|Doctrine of the Mean (Mesotes)|Aristotle: virtue as intermediate between excess and deficiency relative to the agent; courage between rashness and cowardice|ethics
CO34|Ataraxia (Tranquility)|Epicurean and Pyrrhonist goal: undisturbed state of soul; achieved through pleasure management (Epicurus) or suspension of judgment (Pyrrho)|ethics
CO35|Apatheia (Freedom from Passions)|Stoic ideal: not emotionlessness but freedom from irrational passions (pathē); sage acts from reason alone|ethics
CO36|Wu Wei (Non-Action)|Daoist principle: effortless action aligned with natural order (Dao); not passivity but non-forcing; water as metaphor|ethics
CO37|Ren (Humaneness/Benevolence)|Confucian cardinal virtue: compassionate concern for others; root of all virtues; expressed through li (ritual propriety)|ethics
CO38|Li (Ritual Propriety)|Confucianism: proper conduct, ceremonies, social norms; shapes character; externalizes ren; distinct from Neo-Confucian li (principle)|ethics
CO39|Dharma|Hindu/Buddhist: cosmic law, moral duty (Hindu); teaching of the Buddha (Buddhist); natural law governing existence|ethics
CO40|Karma|Hindu/Buddhist/Jain: moral causation; actions produce consequences in this or future lives; binds to samsara; liberation requires exhausting or transcending karma|ethics
CO41|Ahimsa (Non-Violence)|Jain cardinal principle; also Hindu and Buddhist; non-harm to any living being; Jainism: most absolute form including microscopic life|ethics
CO42|Natural Law|universal moral principles derived from nature or reason; Stoics: living according to nature; Aquinas: participation of eternal law in rational creatures|ethics
CO43|Divine Command Theory|morality determined by God's commands; Euthyphro dilemma challenges this: is it good because God commands it, or does God command it because it is good?|ethics
CO44|Episteme (Knowledge)|certain, demonstrable knowledge; Plato: justified true belief (with qualification from Theaetetus); Aristotle: knowledge of causes through demonstration|epistemology
CO45|Doxa (Opinion/Belief)|Plato: inferior to episteme; concerns sensible world; may be true or false but lacks rational justification; divided into eikasia (imagination) and pistis (belief)|epistemology
CO46|Anamnesis (Recollection)|Plato: learning is recollecting what the soul knew before embodiment; demonstrated in Meno with slave boy and geometry|epistemology
CO47|Katalepsis (Cognitive Impression)|Stoic epistemology: impression so clear and distinct it compels assent; criterion of truth; attacked by Academic Skeptics|epistemology
CO48|Epochē (Suspension of Judgment)|Pyrrhonian Skepticism: withhold assent to all non-evident propositions; leads to ataraxia; Sextus Empiricus systematizes|epistemology
CO49|Pramana (Means of Valid Knowledge)|Indian epistemology: perception (pratyaksha), inference (anumana), testimony (shabda), comparison (upamana), postulation (arthapatti), non-apprehension (anupalabdhi); schools differ on which accepted|epistemology
CO50|Dialectic|Socratic: question-and-answer exposing contradictions; Platonic: method ascending from hypotheses to unhypothesized first principle (the Good); Aristotle: reasoning from endoxa (reputable opinions)|epistemology
CO51|Syllogism|Aristotle: deductive argument with two premises and conclusion; three terms; three figures; valid forms (Barbara, Celarent, etc.); foundation of formal logic|logic
CO52|Categories|Aristotle: ten basic classifications of being: substance, quantity, quality, relation, place, time, position, state, action, passion|logic
CO53|Square of Opposition|logical relationships among categorical propositions: contradictory, contrary, subcontrary, subalternation; A, E, I, O forms|logic
CO54|Problem of Universals|do universals exist independently (realism), only in mind (conceptualism), or only as names (nominalism)? Plato, Aristotle, Porphyry, Boethius, medieval debates|metaphysics
CO55|Allegory of the Cave|Plato (Republic VII): prisoners see shadows on wall; freed prisoner ascends to sunlight (Form of the Good); represents education, enlightenment, philosopher's duty to return|epistemology
CO56|Divided Line|Plato (Republic VI): four levels of cognition — eikasia (images), pistis (physical objects), dianoia (mathematical reasoning), noesis (direct apprehension of Forms)|epistemology
CO57|Theory of Recollection|Plato: the soul, being immortal, has knowledge of Forms from prior existence; learning = recovering this knowledge; supports argument for immortality|epistemology
CO58|Analogy of Being (Analogia Entis)|Aquinas: being is predicated of God and creatures not univocally or equivocally but analogically; preserves divine transcendence while allowing rational theology|metaphysics
CO59|Essence and Existence|Aquinas: in all creatures, essence (what it is) is really distinct from existence (that it is); in God alone, essence = existence (ipsum esse subsistens)|metaphysics
CO60|Five Ways|Aquinas: five arguments for God's existence: motion, efficient causation, contingency, gradation of being, teleological governance|theology
CO61|Negative Theology (Via Negativa)|knowing God by what God is not; Pseudo-Dionysius, Maimonides, Aquinas (partially); preserves divine transcendence|theology
CO62|Occasionalism (Islamic)|Al-Ghazali: no natural causation; God directly causes every event; fire does not burn cotton — God creates burning when fire is near cotton|metaphysics
CO63|Maqasid al-Shariah (Objectives of Islamic Law)|preservation of religion, life, intellect, lineage, property; framework for Islamic jurisprudence and ethics|ethics
CO64|Mandate of Heaven (Tianming)|Chinese political philosophy: ruler's legitimacy derives from moral virtue; heaven withdraws mandate from unjust ruler; justifies dynastic change|political
CO65|Philosopher-King|Plato (Republic): only philosophers who know the Good should rule; political authority grounded in knowledge, not wealth or birth|political
CO66|Politeia (Constitution/Republic)|Plato: ideal city-state with three classes (rulers, guardians, producers) mirroring three parts of soul (reason, spirit, appetite)|political
CO67|Mixed Constitution|Polybius, Cicero: best constitution combines monarchy, aristocracy, democracy; prevents degeneration cycle (anacyclosis)|political
CO68|Social Harmony (He)|Confucian political ideal: harmonious society achieved through virtuous leadership, proper relationships (five bonds), and ritual propriety|political
CO69|Just War (Bellum Iustum)|Augustine and Aquinas: war morally permissible under conditions: just cause, legitimate authority, right intention, proportionality, last resort|political
